Who we are

Building a stronger response to diabetes in Somalia

Somali Diabetes Foundation is a nonprofit organization committed to reducing the burden of diabetes through community-centered education, prevention, patient support, partnerships, research and advocacy.

Our work connects communities and healthcare professionals with practical, evidence-informed solutions.

Our Mission

To improve diabetes prevention, early detection, treatment, education and support for communities across Somalia.

Our Vision

A Somalia where people can prevent diabetes, access quality care and live healthier lives with fewer avoidable complications.

What we do

Our priority programs

Our programs are designed around the needs of people at risk of diabetes and people living with diabetes.

Education

Diabetes Awareness

Community education on diabetes risk factors, symptoms, healthy lifestyles, treatment and complication prevention.

Prevention

Screening & Early Detection

Promoting risk assessment, screening and timely referral for earlier diagnosis and intervention.

Care

Patient Support

Helping people and families understand diabetes self-care, medicines, follow-up and complication prevention.

Advocacy

Access to Essential Care

Advocating for equitable access to insulin, medicines, diagnostics and quality diabetes services.

Research

Research & Evidence

Supporting research and data that can strengthen diabetes policy, clinical practice and health programs.

Partnerships

Professional & Community Engagement

Working with professionals, communities, NGOs and partners to create sustainable impact.

Our story

The idea behind SDF grew from a clinical experience that highlighted the devastating intersection of diabetes, poverty and limited access to care.

The organization was officially founded on 20 November 2024 to turn that concern into sustained action.

Why SDF exists

From one experience to a national mission

Diabetes requires more than medical treatment. It requires awareness, prevention, affordable care, patient education, family support, research and strong health systems.

“No person should be left without hope because diabetes care is out of reach.”
